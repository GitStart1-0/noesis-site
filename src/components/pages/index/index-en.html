How the content works

An answer is only the beginning

Most quiz apps end the interaction immediately after an answer: right or wrong, the score is recorded and the next question appears. Mythos: Quiz adds one more step: an explanation shown regardless of the result1, together with a source for the fact2.

Some tasks go beyond a single correct answer. Matching, arranging sequences and comparing parallel texts ask you to connect information rather than simply recall it3. This approach is close to the methods used in general academic aptitude tests, designed to assess analytical thinking in admissions examinations4.

Example

“Why does a compass usually keep its orientation during a solar storm, but sometimes deflect?”

Explanation: a needle responds to the local magnetic field of Earth, which usually keeps its main direction. Strong geomagnetic storms can still cause measurable field variation and deflect the needle, especially at high latitudes.

Source: U.S. Geological Survey, Geomagnetism Program.

Content is reviewed by a specialist in the relevant field before publication5. This cannot eliminate every mistake, but it moves most corrections into review rather than into user comments after release.

  1. Explanations appear immediately after an answer, regardless of whether it is correct.
  2. Sources include open scientific archives and peer-reviewed publications.
  3. Formats include matching, ordering and comparing texts A/B.
  4. Comparable methods include LSAT Comparative Reading, LNAT and general academic aptitude tests.
  5. Review takes place before a level is published, not after user complaints.
